The Meadow

The most valuable thing here is a field, and it is free.

Ninety-eight hectares of restored hay meadow, begun in 1994 with green hay from a surviving meadow eight miles away. It is cut once a year and grazed after, and that is the entire management.

    The cut

    Once, in the last week of July.

    Not before, because the yellow rattle has to set and drop. Not after, because the sward goes over and the aftermath will not come. One date, and it decides the next hundred years.

    The hay is lifted within four days and goes to the dairy. Cattle come on in the middle of August and stay until the ground turns, treading seed in as they go. Then nothing at all until the following July.

    1. May to July

      Left entirely alone. Paths mown, everything else standing.

    2. Last week of July

      Cut, turned twice, baled and lifted inside four days.

    3. Mid-August to October

      Aftermath grazing. Roughly one cow to the hectare.

    4. November to April

      Nothing. No feeding, no topping, no rolling.
